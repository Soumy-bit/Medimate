Ways to pay less

  • Ask about the generic. A medicine with the same active ingredient and strength is often much cheaper.
  • Jan Aushadhi Kendras sell generic medicines at lower prices. janaushadhi.gov.in
  • Price limits exist. You should never pay more than the MRP printed on the pack, and the government (NPPA) sets ceiling prices for scheduled essential medicines. nppaindia.nic.in
  • Do not buy more than you can use before the expiry date. A bigger strip can cost less per tablet, but only if you will finish it in time.
  • Stay safe. Buy from licensed pharmacies, keep the bill, check the seal and expiry date, and use a valid prescription for prescription-only medicines.
Search a brand name (like Dolo or Pan) or the salt name printed on the strip (like paracetamol). MediMate shows the active ingredient and other brands that contain it. It never says what dose to take, and it does not guess: a medicine that is not in its list is left out.

Before you switch brands

  • Match all three: the same active ingredient(s), the same strength, and the same form (tablet, capsule, syrup, inhaler). Slow-release labels such as SR, ER or XR are not the same as plain tablets.
  • Read the strip. “Each tablet contains…” lists the salts. That is the real answer, more than any list.
  • Ask first for thyroid medicine, blood thinners, anti-seizure medicines, heart-rhythm medicines, and anything for a child or in pregnancy. For these, small differences matter more.
  • Why generics cost less: the active ingredient is the same. The price difference usually comes from branding and marketing.
  • Never swap to a different medicine because it “looks similar”. Only your doctor or pharmacist can do that.

Can I donate?

  • Usually age 18 to 65 and at least 45 kg.
  • Haemoglobin is checked on the day. Blood banks usually need about 12.5 g/dL or more.
  • You should feel well: no fever, cold or infection.
  • Usual gap between donations: 3 months for men, 4 months for women.
  • A recent tattoo, piercing, surgery, vaccination, pregnancy, breastfeeding or some medicines can mean waiting.

These are typical Indian blood bank rules. The doctor at the blood bank decides on the day. National Blood Transfusion Council
